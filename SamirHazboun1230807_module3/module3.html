Mass, damper and spring

Model and formulas from the lecture

Transfer function. G(s) = 1/(ms² + bs + k) = Kωₙ²/(s² + 2ζωₙs + ωₙ²).

Parameters. K = 1/k is the static gain, ωₙ = √(k/m) is the natural frequency, and ζ = b/(2√(mk)) is the damping ratio. The slides call ζ the damping coefficient. Here b is the physical damper constant.

Step response. The formulas below assume zero initial displacement and velocity. Astep is the force-step amplitude (1 N). The graph shows y(t)/(KAstep), so the final value is 1 on the graph.

Underdamped. y(t)/(KAstep) = 1 − e−δt sin(ωdt + φ)/√(1 − ζ²), where δ = ζωₙ, ωd = ωₙ√(1 − ζ²) and φ = arccos(ζ).

Critically damped. y(t)/(KAstep) = 1 − (1 + ωₙt)e−ωₙt.

Overdamped. y(t)/(KAstep) = 1 − (s₂es₁t − s₁es₂t)/(s₂ − s₁), with real poles s₁, s₂ = −ζωₙ ± ωₙ√(ζ² − 1).

Underdamped peak. tp = π/ωd. The fractional overshoot is Mp = e−πζ/√(1 − ζ²). Multiply by 100 for percent overshoot.

Underdamped first crossing of the final value. The formula on slide 68 uses the first positive angle solving tan(ωdt) = −ωd/δ. This gives (π − φ)/ωd .
